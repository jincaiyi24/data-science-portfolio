# Sentence Validation Codebook for SSCI Submission

## Purpose

This codebook defines how to manually validate the sentence-level digital strategy measures used in the study.

The purpose is to prove that the text-based variables are not simple keyword counts. Instead, they identify whether annual-report sentences contain substantive digital strategy actions, business model reconstruction, capital-market-visible signals, or generic digital rhetoric.

## Unit of Annotation

The unit of annotation is one English annual-report sentence.

Each sampled sentence should be linked to:

- corp_code
- stock_code
- corp_name
- year
- rcept_no
- sentence_id
- sentence text

## Required Labels

### Label 1: Digital Sentence

Variable name:

```text
manual_digital_sentence
```

Definition:

A sentence is a digital sentence if it discusses digital technology, digital transformation, data, AI, cloud, platforms, software, automation, smart factories, online channels, connected products, or similar digital concepts in a corporate strategy, operation, product, service, or market context.

Code:

```text
1 = digital sentence
0 = not a digital sentence
```

Positive examples:

- The company invested in an AI-based data analytics platform to optimize production planning.
- We expanded our cloud-based software service for enterprise customers.
- The group introduced smart factory systems across its major production sites.

Negative examples:

- The company held an online meeting with shareholders.
- The annual report is available in digital format.
- Contact information is provided on the company website.

### Label 2: Substantive Digital Strategy Sentence

Variable name:

```text
manual_substantive_digital_sentence
```

Definition:

A sentence is substantive if it describes a concrete digital action, investment, implementation, product/service launch, operational change, platform development, data use, or business process transformation.

Code:

```text
1 = substantive digital strategy sentence
0 = non-substantive or generic digital sentence
```

Positive examples:

- We deployed AI inspection systems to improve production yield.
- The company launched a data-driven subscription service for connected vehicles.
- We built an integrated digital platform to connect suppliers, distributors, and customers.

Negative examples:

- Digital transformation is important for future growth.
- We will respond to the digital era.
- The company recognizes digital innovation as a key trend.

Decision rule:

If a sentence only states aspiration, importance, trend awareness, or generic commitment, code 0.

### Label 3: BMI-Related Digital Sentence

Variable name:

```text
manual_bmi_sentence
```

Definition:

A sentence is BMI-related if the digital content changes or supports the firm's business model, including products, services, revenue model, customer interface, distribution channel, ecosystem, platform strategy, or value proposition.

Code:

```text
1 = business-model-reconstruction digital sentence
0 = not BMI-related
```

Positive indicators:

- new digital service
- digital platform
- software service
- data service
- subscription
- recurring revenue
- platform revenue
- online channel
- customer experience
- ecosystem
- smart product
- mobility service
- connected service

Positive examples:

- We launched a subscription-based software service to generate recurring revenue.
- The company expanded its online channel and personalized customer experience platform.
- Connected services became a new business model for our mobility division.

Negative examples:

- We upgraded internal IT security systems.
- We adopted digital tools to improve administrative efficiency.
- We installed automation equipment in factories without changing products, services, channels, or revenue logic.

Decision rule:

Operational efficiency alone is not BMI unless it clearly changes customer value, offering, channel, ecosystem, or revenue model.

### Label 4: Market-Visible Digital Signal

Variable name:

```text
manual_market_signal_sentence
```

Definition:

A sentence is a market-visible digital signal if it communicates digital strategy, digital capability, or digital transformation in a way that outside investors can use to infer future growth, competitiveness, innovation, or strategic direction.

Code:

```text
1 = market-visible digital signal
0 = not a capital-market-relevant digital signal
```

Positive examples:

- The company plans to expand its AI platform business as a new growth engine.
- Our digital healthcare service is expected to strengthen long-term revenue growth.
- We are accelerating software-defined vehicle services to create future mobility value.

Negative examples:

- Employees used digital forms for internal administration.
- The report was distributed through an online system.
- A website address is shown in the annual report.

Decision rule:

The sentence should be visible and meaningful to outside investors, not merely an internal administrative detail.

### Label 5: Boilerplate or Hype Sentence

Variable name:

```text
manual_boilerplate_or_hype
```

Definition:

A sentence is boilerplate or hype if it uses digital language but lacks concrete action, implementation, investment, business model change, measurable progress, or verifiable substance.

Code:

```text
1 = boilerplate / hype
0 = not boilerplate / hype
```

Positive examples:

- Digital transformation is an important trend.
- We will continue to strive for digital innovation.
- The company will respond proactively to the digital era.

Negative examples:

- We invested KRW 50 billion in smart factory automation and data integration.
- The company launched a cloud-based analytics platform for logistics customers.
- Subscription revenue from digital services increased during the year.

Decision rule:

Generic future-oriented language without concrete action should be coded as hype/boilerplate.

## Recommended Sampling Design

Minimum SSCI-acceptable validation:

```text
500 sentences
```

Preferred validation:

```text
1000 sentences
```

Sampling should include:

- sentences classified by the algorithm as substantive digital
- sentences classified as BMI-related
- sentences classified as boilerplate
- sentences not classified as digital, for false-negative testing
- multiple industries
- multiple years
- both high and low DigitalStrategy firms

Recommended stratified sample:

```text
250 algorithm-positive substantive digital sentences
250 algorithm-positive BMI or market signal sentences
250 algorithm-positive boilerplate sentences
250 algorithm-negative sentences
```

## Validation Metrics

For each label, calculate:

```text
Precision = true positives / algorithm positives
Recall = true positives / manual positives
F1 = 2 * precision * recall / (precision + recall)
```

If two human coders are available, calculate:

```text
Cohen's Kappa
```

Target threshold:

- Precision above 0.80 is desirable.
- F1 above 0.75 is acceptable for rule-based text measurement.
- Cohen's Kappa above 0.70 is desirable.

If the first validation result is weak, revise the dictionary and re-run the classifier before final regressions.

## Annotation Notes

Annotators should not infer information outside the sentence unless the sentence explicitly refers to a clear digital action.

When uncertain:

- code substantive digital sentence as 0 unless the sentence contains a clear action or implementation
- code BMI as 1 only when product, service, channel, customer interface, ecosystem, platform, or revenue logic is affected
- code hype as 1 when language is broad, aspirational, and not verifiable

## How This Supports the Paper

The validation results will be used in the methods section to argue that:

1. The measure reduces keyword-count measurement error.
2. The algorithm distinguishes substantive digital strategy from generic digital rhetoric.
3. The BMI and market-signal paths are empirically separable.
4. The hype penalty is grounded in observed mismatch between external signal and internal substance.
